# State Bank of Vietnam: Monetary Policy Summary 2024

**Document type:** Educational policy summary  
**Primary institution:** State Bank of Vietnam (SBV)  
**Reference:** https://www.sbv.gov.vn/  
**Coverage:** Monetary and banking policy during 2024

## Policy objectives and operating stance

The State Bank of Vietnam managed monetary policy flexibly to control inflation, support macroeconomic stability and facilitate a recovery in economic growth. Its operating challenge was to balance several objectives at once: credit availability, exchange-rate stability, banking-system safety and inflation control. The SBV kept key policy rates unchanged through 2024 after the reductions made in 2023. The refinancing rate remained 4.5% per year, the discount rate 3.0%, and the overnight electronic interbank lending rate 5.0%.

## Credit policy

The system-wide credit growth orientation for 2024 was announced early in the year at about 15%, with allocations communicated to credit institutions. Actual credit growth reached approximately 15.08% by year-end. The policy aimed to direct lending toward production, business activity and priority sectors while restricting credit to risky areas. Commercial banks were encouraged to reduce operating costs and lending rates, but lending decisions still had to meet credit standards and risk-management requirements.

## Exchange rate and liquidity

The Vietnamese dong faced depreciation pressure as the US dollar strengthened and international interest-rate conditions remained restrictive. The SBV used central-rate management, foreign-exchange operations and liquidity instruments to smooth excessive volatility rather than promise a fixed exchange rate. Open-market operations and bill issuance helped manage short-term banking liquidity. Exchange-rate intervention can absorb domestic currency liquidity, so liquidity and foreign-exchange actions must be interpreted together.

## Banking stability

Policy implementation also emphasized the restructuring of weak credit institutions, handling bad debts and improving banking governance. System-wide credit expansion does not imply that every borrower or sector receives credit equally. Asset quality can react with a lag after periods of economic weakness, and reported non-performing loan ratios do not capture every restructured or potentially impaired exposure.

## Transmission and limitations

Lower funding costs can support credit demand and investment, but transmission depends on borrower confidence, collateral, expected sales and banks' risk appetite. Therefore, unchanged policy rates do not mean that monetary conditions were inactive: credit targets, liquidity operations, foreign-exchange management and supervisory measures also shaped financial conditions. Monetary easing has limits when firms lack viable demand or when balance-sheet risks constrain lending.
